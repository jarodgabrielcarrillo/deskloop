Free GPA Calculator — No Signup Required

The Deskloop GPA Calculator tracks your academic performance across every semester with no account required. Add courses, assign credit hours, select your grade, and your cumulative GPA updates live. All data stays in your browser — nothing is ever sent to a server.

How GPA is calculated

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours. Quality points per course = Grade Points × Credit Hours. An A (4.0) in a 3-credit course contributes 12 quality points toward your cumulative average.

What-If Grade Simulator

Toggle What-If Mode to test how changing any grade would affect your GPA — without overwriting your saved data. Useful for deciding whether to retake a course or which final exams matter most.

Pass/Fail courses

Mark any course as Pass or Fail. Pass adds credits toward your total without affecting GPA. Fail (No Credit) removes both — matching how most universities treat P/F grading.

Multiple grading scales

Supports 4.0, 4.3 (with A+), 5.0, and 100-point scales. Switch at any time and all grades recalculate instantly. The scale selector also updates the standing thresholds automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

On a 4.0 scale: 3.5+ is generally considered Dean's List territory and is competitive for most graduate programs. 3.0–3.49 is good standing. 2.5–2.99 is satisfactory. Below 2.0 typically triggers academic probation. However, "good" is relative to your field — engineering programs often have lower median GPAs than liberal arts programs due to grading curves.
Most graduate programs have a minimum GPA requirement of 3.0, but competitive programs at top universities typically admit students with 3.5+ GPAs. Medical schools (MD) typically want 3.7+. Law schools look heavily at LSAT scores alongside GPA. PhD programs in STEM often focus more on research experience than GPA, though 3.3+ is usually expected. Always check specific program requirements.
GPA improvement is harder the more credits you already have — early grades have more weight because there are fewer total credits. Strategies: retake courses where your school allows grade replacement, take higher-credit courses where you can perform well (each credit matters more), use the What-If simulator to identify which courses to prioritize, and consider taking electives in subjects where you have a competitive advantage.
Latin honors thresholds vary by institution, but a common standard is: Cum Laude (3.5–3.64), Magna Cum Laude (3.65–3.79), Summa Cum Laude (3.8–4.0). Some schools use class rank instead of fixed GPA thresholds. Check your institution's specific requirements — they may also require a minimum number of credits completed at that institution.
At most universities, a Pass grade does not affect your GPA — it adds the credits to your total without contributing grade points, making it mathematically neutral. A Fail (No Credit) also doesn't affect GPA in most institutions, but you lose the credits. Some graduate programs and professional schools may ask for the breakdown of P/F courses separately, so it's worth tracking them. Use the P/F toggle in this calculator to mark courses accordingly.
GPA (Grade Point Average) can refer to any period — a semester, a year, or your full academic career. CGPA (Cumulative GPA) specifically refers to your overall GPA across all completed semesters. When most people and institutions say "GPA," they typically mean your cumulative GPA. Semester GPA is your average for that term only and is useful for tracking improvement but doesn't appear on your transcript as prominently as your cumulative figure.
