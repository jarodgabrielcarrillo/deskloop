How your rate is calculated

Your rate is built from the total annual revenue you need to earn — your take-home income goal, plus all business expenses, taxes, and a buffer for slow months. That total is divided by your actual billable hours per year, accounting for working weeks, daily hours, and your utilization rate. The result is the minimum hourly rate that makes your income goal achievable.

Why most freelancers undercharge

Most freelancers set rates based on what feels reasonable, what they charged last year, or what a competitor charges. None of these account for the real cost of self-employment: higher taxes, business expenses, unpaid admin time, and gaps between clients. This calculator forces the actual math.

Understanding utilization rate

Utilization rate is the percentage of your available hours that you actually bill. Admin work, writing proposals, marketing, and chasing invoices all take time no client pays for. Setting this realistically — 60 to 75% for most freelancers — gives you a rate that holds up in practice, not just on paper.

Market rate comparison

The calculator shows where your rate sits relative to market benchmarks for your experience level and location. Rates vary significantly by region: a mid-level developer in the Philippines commands very different rates than one in the US or UK, even for identical remote work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the freelance rate calculator determine my hourly rate?

Enter your target annual income, your billable hours per week, weeks worked per year, and your business expenses. The calculator works backward from your income goal to the hourly rate you need to charge to reach it — accounting for taxes, expenses, and the reality that not every working hour is billable.

Why do billable hours matter so much?

Most freelancers work 40 hours a week but bill far fewer. Admin, proposals, client communication, and professional development consume real time that no client pays for. If you work 40 hours but only bill 25, your effective rate needs to cover all 40 hours of your time — not just the 25 on the invoice.

Can I calculate a project rate instead of an hourly rate?

Yes. Enter your hourly rate and estimated project hours and the calculator outputs a project fee. Add a buffer percentage for scope creep and the fee adjusts accordingly. Compare your project rate against market benchmarks to check whether your pricing is competitive for your niche and experience level.

Does it account for taxes?

Yes. Enter your estimated tax rate and the calculator factors it into your required gross income. Freelancers typically pay both income tax and self-employment tax, which adds up to significantly more than an equivalent salaried employee pays. The calculator surfaces the gross rate you need before tax to hit your net income target.

How do I know if my rate is competitive?

The calculator includes market rate benchmarks by niche — development, design, writing, consulting, and more. Your calculated rate appears alongside typical ranges for your field so you can see whether you're pricing below, within, or above market. Pricing below market often signals that your billable hour estimate is too optimistic.

Can I save different rate profiles?

Yes. Save multiple profiles — one for your primary niche, one for a secondary service, one for a specific client type. Switch between profiles to compare the rates different service lines require. All profiles save to localStorage with no account needed.

Completely free. No account, no email, no subscription. Open the page, enter your numbers, and get your rate in seconds. Saved profiles store in your browser and nothing leaves your device.
