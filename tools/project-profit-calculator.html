Project Profit Calculator Free — Know Your Real Freelance Margin

Revenue is not profit

When a client pays you $5,000 that is revenue. Subtract the hours you spent at your target rate plus every direct expense you paid to deliver the project and you get actual profit. Most freelancers discover their effective rate is far below what they assumed when they priced the work.

Break-even analysis for freelancers

The calculator shows three break-even figures: the minimum viable rate to cover all expenses with zero profit, the rate needed to hit a 30% margin, and the maximum hours you can work before the project stops being profitable. All three update instantly as you change any input.

What-if scenarios with before and after

Three sliders let you explore scenarios side by side. Increase revenue by a percentage, reduce hours by a percentage, or model an expense overrun. Each slider shows the exact profit before and after so the impact is visible without doing mental math.

Compare saved projects

Save up to 6 projects. Click one to load it. Click a second to see a side-by-side comparison of revenue, profit, margin, and effective rate. Useful for identifying which types of work generate the best returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the project profit calculator measure?

It calculates your actual profit from a project after accounting for all costs — time, tools, software, subcontractors, taxes, and platform fees. Enter your project rate and expenses, and the calculator shows your gross revenue, total costs, net profit, profit margin, and effective hourly rate.

Why does my effective hourly rate matter?

Your quoted project rate means little without knowing how many hours you actually spent. A $2,000 project that takes 80 hours pays $25 per hour. The same project at 20 hours pays $100 per hour. The effective hourly rate tells you which projects are genuinely worth your time and which ones erode your income.

Can I include platform fees and taxes?

Yes. Add platform fees as a percentage — useful for Upwork, Fiverr, or payment processor fees. Add a tax rate and the calculator separates your pre-tax and post-tax profit. Every line item appears as a separate deduction so you see exactly where revenue disappears.

Can I save multiple projects for comparison?

Yes. Save any project as a named entry and compare multiple projects side by side. Useful for reviewing which client types or project categories produce the strongest margins over time.

How is profit margin calculated?

Profit margin equals net profit divided by gross revenue, expressed as a percentage. A project that earns $1,500 revenue with $500 in costs has a 66.7% profit margin. Most freelancers target margins above 60% to maintain a sustainable income after accounting for unpaid admin time.

Does this work for fixed-price and hourly projects?

Yes. For fixed-price projects, enter the total fee and your estimated hours. For hourly projects, enter your rate and the actual hours logged. Both modes calculate the same profit metrics so you can compare fixed and hourly work on equal terms.

Is the calculator free with no sign-up?

Completely free. No account required. Open the page, enter your numbers, and get your profit breakdown instantly. Saved projects store in your browser's localStorage and nothing leaves your device.
